# Holiday Tavern cast (shared by the charcoal and flash looks)

From Bob's notes on draft 1 (2026-10-06): the narrator is a **young man among older people, with no
suggestion they're family**. In draft 1 the figures read as parents and a child. That must not happen again.

The same people appear in both looks, so a viewer recognises them across the cut at 130.0 s.

| Who | Look | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **The kid** (narrator) | About 21. Slim, a little gangly, messy dark mop of hair, clean-shaven, red-and-white striped T-shirt, jeans, sneakers. Adult proportions: head about 1/7 of his height, never the big-head child build. | Generic young man, not a likeness of Bob. In charcoal the stripes are grey bands. |
| **The tall guy** (the offer, piano in C) | Lean, slicked quiff, sunglasses at night, black shirt. 30s, smug. | Verse A. |
| **Howard** | Late 40s. Balding with a combover, saggy leather jacket, cigarette. | Host of the place. Shabby, not menacing. |
| **Maria** | Late 40s. Frizzy hair with grey streaks, heavy eyeliner, too many bracelets, cardigan. | Never maternal: no hugging him, no hand-holding, no hovering beside him as a pair with Howard. |
| **Bazil** | 50s. Paunchy, scraggly beard, stained cardigan or jean jacket. | Comic and useless. |
| **Gregory** (the loft) | Drawn after young Greg Keelor (Bob asked for it). Reference: `source/ref/greg-keelor.jpg (1080x1080, Blue Rodeo photo Bob linked)`. Swept-back dark hair with one lock falling over his forehead, lean long face, strong jaw, an embroidered western shirt with a piped yoke, a bolo tie. About 30. | A caricature in the look's own technique, not a photoreal portrait. He is the one who falls asleep in the wicker chair. |
| Older folks freaking out (verse B) | Howard, Maria and Bazil themselves. | Burnt-out hangers-on who are scared he might die and pathetic at the same time (Bob's words). |

**Staging rules against the family read:**
- No two older figures framing him symmetrically, one on each side.
- No adult hand on his head, and no leading him by the hand.
- Never an older man and woman bending over him together.
- They hold him up the way drunk friends do: an arm slung over a shoulder, clumsy, cigarettes still in their hands.
- He is never shorter than about 90% of their height.
