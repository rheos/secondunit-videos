# All The Trees — alchemical engraving look test

Issue #119; song clock 47.000–79.500 s, 24 fps, 780 frames. This is a look approval cut, not approval for a complete film.

The key image is two engraved people whose hands become boughs and whose roots join below ground. Above the soil they remain distinct; below it their relationship becomes visible. The sun and moon are faces, not decorations attached to faces. This takes Mark Von Ulrich's bodily metaphor and physical relationships into the emblem tradition; his early-Mac rendering is not used here.

Warm cream paper, brown-black copperplate lines, azure sky, ultramarine and rose robes, gamboge sun, sap-green leaf washes. No brown vignette. Colour sits under transparent ink; uneven wash boundaries leave paper visible. Real source engraving is the figure language. Modest robes repair the original nude figures; that repair is disclosed in SOURCES.md.

A permanent pair of cloud heralds answers the brass. Hands and instruments articulate at local joints. Human arms rise into tree boughs; the bodies do not pivot like rigid dolls. Birds gain halos and carry small trumpets, land on the hands, depart, then one returns. Three roundels make wind, sun and winter into events with distinct motion. Roots physically meet at the centre.

The motion clock is deterministic song time. Character poses update on twos; the camera and wash reveal can move every frame. No accumulated simulation, random calls in the render loop, time-based CSS, or previous-frame dependence. A slow camera and drifting cut-out clouds keep the plate alive, while specific actions carry the words and note attacks.

Protect: the joined-root thesis, source faces, fine linework, bright colour, continuous brass presence. Reject: generic Victorian collage, doll proportions, whole-body rocking, brown-on-brown grading, cartoon vector people, early-Mac pixels, unreadable details.
