Life and career

Nakamura was born in Los Angeles, graduated from ArtCenter College of Design in Pasadena, and later taught there. An exact birth date is not established in this profile. New Mexico PBS, ¡COLORES! artist profile

He grew up in Whittier and later moved to Santa Fe. The 2010 profile identifies him as a third-generation Japanese American and connects his cultural interests to his family’s experience of assimilation. Mia Nakaji Monnier, Discover Nikkei, June 22, 2010

By 1985, he was teaching at ArtCenter and producing commercial illustrations for Atari and Epson. Los Angeles Times, February 24, 1985

His official biography places him in Santa Fe and describes a practice spanning commercial illustration and gallery work. It lists Time, U.S. News & World Report, the Los Angeles Times, and the 2002 Winter Olympic ceremony programs among his commissions. Official biography

His books include Go West, I Dreamed I Was a Dog, and Imaginary Creatures. The books page describes interconnected creatures and dream journeys as subjects of the work. Official books page

Materials and cultural context

The 2010 interview describes polymer painting on tin and discusses Southwestern retablos, petroglyphs, mythology, Japanese popular culture, and his interest in reconnecting with Japanese traditions. These are specific influences, not a single interchangeable cultural style. Mia Nakaji Monnier, Discover Nikkei, June 22, 2010

The portfolio also records acrylic on canvas and work on hand-tooled panels. The medium should be established separately for each piece. Artist portfolio: Neo-Folk Primitive

What he says about developing an image

In the 2010 interview, he says: “I like to begin with a story to build around.” Mia Nakaji Monnier, Discover Nikkei, June 22, 2010

In his Design by Nature contribution, he recalls museum visits with his art-educator parents and an early encounter with Hieronymus Bosch. He describes surrounding himself with books and reference material until a concept forms, and broadening what he shows clients to include his sketches and doodles. Painting, he says, is a sustained process. Maggie Macnab, Design by Nature, publisher excerpt, copyright 2012; printed pp. 12–13
