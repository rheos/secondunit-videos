JOEL NAKAMURA — MATERIAL AND COMPOSITION REFERENCE
Updated 2026-10-13

SCOPE
This guide describes features observed in the downloaded references, with a suggested code-rendering approach. It is not a recovered technical recipe. The collection spans several treatments: painted and ornamented panels, open illustrative compositions, and flatter arrangements of glyph-like figures. Select the relevant family before rendering.

MATERIAL EVIDENCE
Published profiles describe polymer paint on tin, and current portfolio records also identify acrylic on canvas and hand-tooled panels. Medium varies by work. Do not assign tin or a particular paint to a file without a source that identifies it. The irregular edges and patterned frames in the references can inform a material study without establishing the material of every example.

VISIBLE QUALITIES
Use a strong, readable silhouette containing a second level of activity: inhabitants, small objects, landscapes, diagrams, or repeated symbols. Heads, animals, vessels, and instruments can each become a setting.

Many works use frontal, emblem-like figures, oval enclosures, arcs, spirals, and borders. Other works use side views, scenes, or scattered glyphs. Keep a coherent hierarchy rather than requiring symmetry in every piece.

Color can be saturated and luminous: blue or violet grounds against yellow, orange, red, or green figures. Tone often changes gradually within a shape. Outlines and local halos separate overlapping forms. Light can appear to come from an object, with a concentrated bright center or glow around a contour.

Fine marks, short repeated strokes, dotted fields, and small pictorial forms fill surfaces. Inspect a higher-resolution reference before treating JPEG noise or scanning artifacts as intentional marks. Preserve clean focal shapes within the dense detail.

When a frame belongs to the selected treatment, give it the presence of a made object: uneven contour, patterned rim, alternating dents or raised marks, restrained highlights and shadow. Use the frame to contain or echo the scene.

PROPOSED CODE APPROACH
Separate silhouette, internal scene, contour, fine pattern, and panel/frame into layers. Author marks in each object's local coordinates. Use deterministic seeds for irregularity so a surface retains its identity when moved. Material highlights should follow the geometry of the imagined surface; a metal rim and painted interior need different responses to light.

Use enough resolution for small inhabitants and fine marks. Choose working dimensions according to detail and display size. There is no established 72-dpi, 256-color, or ordered-dither requirement for Nakamura in these sources. Those belong to Robin's separate Von Ulrich reconstruction brief.

ANIMATION EXTENSION
Let an internal action affect its container, or let a visible path carry something between figures. Keep surface marks attached to objects. Give reactions time to read. These are proposed motion principles, not claims about Nakamura's animation practice.

ACCEPTANCE
- At small size, the main character and action remain legible.
- At close range, the second layer rewards inspection.
- The chosen material has consistent edges, marks, and light.
- Texture enriches authored forms; it does not substitute for them.
- A proposed animation explains a relationship or change.
- The symbolism remains useful even when the rendering medium changes.
