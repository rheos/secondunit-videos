# Study: Mark Von Ulrich, and why Travel Agent's nested-phone shot recalls him (2026-10-13)

Robin's favourite shot in Travel Agent draft 5 is e_droste (6:15-6:19): a big frontal face, eyes and brows only,
two hands holding a phone sideways across the mouth; in the screen, the agent behind bars peering over her monitor,
which shows him again. These notes say what that shot shares with Von Ulrich's work (13 pieces in this folder), what
it doesn't, and what could carry into the film WITHOUT breaking its one style (cream hatching vs navy blueprint).
Observations only; nothing here is a statement by the artist.

## What the shot shares with him (the grammar, not the look)

1. **The device as the head.** U03 "Let's Get Physical": the head IS a monitor with one eye; headphones frame it.
   U01 "Machine Elves": a framed black screen is the face of a factory-building. In e_droste the phone replaces his
   mouth and lower face: the person is partly made of the thing that mediates him. That is the core echo.
2. **Frontal, symmetrical, iconic.** His figures face us square-on, mirror-balanced, centred, like emblems
   (U01, U03, U04, U09-U13). Our shot is the same: dead-centre face, two hands as matching brackets, a centred
   rectangle. Most of Travel Agent is the opposite: a small three-quarter figure on a side-on ground.
3. **Frame inside frame, container inside container.** U01's screen within flanking towers; U02 "This goes into
   this": a house hangs over a head over a burst. e_droste's recursion (him > phone > cage > her > monitor > him) is
   the same nesting idea pushed to infinity.
4. **The mask-like face.** Two dots, flat brows, no nose: his faces are built from a few geometric parts
   (U09-U13: one face reused, with only the hair changing to say something). Our traveller is the same kind of
   face at that scale.
5. **Scale used as meaning.** U06: a giant ear with a tiny man beside it ("listening" dominates). Our best act D
   shots do this (the head with rooms in it, the heart as a hill).
6. **Hands as framing devices.** U02 raised hands either side, U04 hands on the instrument at the frame edge,
   U08 a hand carrying eyes. In e_droste the hands make the frame.

## What is his and stays out (Robin's one-style rule)

Saturated primaries (red, yellow, cyan, deep blue) on black or white; smooth gradients inside hard shapes;
deliberately jagged low-res raster edges (he drew in SuperPaint and kept the 72 dpi pixelation); radiating
spike-bursts and zigzag fields as decoration; right-angle tube limbs. Travel Agent's look is muted warm palette,
hatching only, no gradients, doubled ink, blueprint navy. (Correction: he died about ten years ago; Robin is a fan who wants to keep his vision alive without copying his
work.) Robin's decision for draft 6: the Ngo look stays; his surface (saturated colour, pixel edge) lives only inside
screens as another world; his thinking shapes the drawn shots. See the project's MVU-DIRECTION.md.

## What could carry into Travel Agent, inside its own look

- **More frontal "emblem" shots.** The film critic found the strongest shots break the default three-quarter
  composition; Von Ulrich's square-on symmetry is a ready grammar for them. Candidates among shots draft 6 is
  re-staging anyway (no protected shot touched).
- **More device-as-body images from the lyrics.** "Help me quick": his face is the payphone's dial. "I've got to
  know": the typing dots as his eyes. "Hear the sun out loud": an ear-phone (U06's ear, drawn in hatching).
  "Mind's the snow": his head as the snow globe (already close). Each is a new picture, so it also helps the
  no-repeat rule.
- **A field-guide chart of his head, U09-U13 style.** One schematic insert: the same traveller face five times,
  only the hat/head changing per state (BLUES / ON HOLD / BOOKED / CAGED / SNOW), labelled like Think:Act's chart.
  It fits the film's field-guide frame exactly and is a unique schematic.
- **Bursts as change, in blueprint mode only.** His spike-bursts mark energy; the film already reserves magenta
  nuclei for change. A magenta radiating burst could mark the agent's form changing at each chorus.
