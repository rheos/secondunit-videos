Terry Gilliam
Biography

An American cartoonist who cut up Britain's respectable past, then spent forty years filming dreamers against systems.

Early life

Born Terrence Vance Gilliam on 22 November 1940 in Minneapolis, Minnesota, to Beatrice (nee Vance) and James Hall Gilliam. His father sold coffee for Folgers, then became a carpenter. The family lived at Medicine Lake, Minnesota, and moved to Panorama City, Los Angeles, in 1952. Terry Gilliam, Wikipedia (read 2026-10-15)

As a teenager he read Mad, then edited by Harvey Kurtzman. In a 2004 interview he said Mad 'inspired everything we ever did'. Terry Gilliam, Wikipedia (read 2026-10-15)

In 2015 he named Mad the magazine of his youth and called Kurtzman and his colleagues really brilliant. He also loved the Sunday comics, especially Milton Caniff's Terry and the Pirates, and copied them. Alex Denney, The art that makes Terry Gilliam tick, Dazed, 10 October 2015

The official Python biography says drawing cartoons shaped his school life and his sceptical attitude toward authority. Terry Gilliam, official Monty Python biography

He graduated from Occidental College in 1962 with a BA in political science. Terry Gilliam, Wikipedia (read 2026-10-15)

Magazines, then London

He worked as a magazine illustrator and art director in Los Angeles and New York in the 1960s, including on Kurtzman's Help!. A photo strip (fumetti) he made for Help! featured John Cleese, which is how they met. Terry Gilliam, official Monty Python biography

When Help! folded he went to Europe. Terry Gilliam, Wikipedia (read 2026-10-15)

One biography says he left the US partly because he feared being drawn into militant politics, after repeated police stops by officers who assumed he used drugs. Not checked against his own account; treat as unconfirmed. Terry Gilliam biography, IMDb

In London he made cartoons and animation for We Have Ways of Making You Laugh, Do Not Adjust Your Set, Marty and Broaden Your Mind. Terry Gilliam, official Monty Python biography

He became a British citizen in 1968, held dual citizenship for 38 years, and renounced US citizenship in January 2006. He described it as a protest against George W. Bush, and elsewhere linked it to tax liability for his family. Terry Gilliam, Wikipedia (read 2026-10-15)

How the method started

For a 1968 Christmas segment of Do Not Adjust Your Set he went to the Tate, found its collection of Victorian Christmas cards, photocopied them and rearranged them. He said the style came out of doing it the quickest, easiest way, with pictures he loved, not from a plan. Lee Thomas, Watch Terry Gilliam's animated short film 'The Christmas Card', Far Out, 24 December 2021

The stream-of-consciousness style of his Do Not Adjust Your Set work impressed Terry Jones, who encouraged him to bring it to Flying Circus. The Absurd and Outrageous Animations of Terry Gilliam, Flavorwire

On the BBC's Do-It-Yourself Film Animation Show (1974) he said the point of animation is to tell a story, make a joke or express an idea, that cut-outs are the easiest form he knows, and that sudden moves are what paper does best. Terry Gilliam on cutout animation, The Do-It-Yourself Film Animation Show (Bob Godfrey, BBC, 1974). BBC Archive clip

He said in 2010 that he used whatever he could get cheaply or free, and that when he ran out of ideas he walked around the National Gallery and came out with a flood of them within minutes. Terry Gilliam revela el mundo surrealista del arte Monty Python, Expansión / CNN México, 9 December 2010

Monty Python

He joined Monty Python's Flying Circus (BBC, 1969-1974) from the start, credited first as animator and later as a full member. His cartoons linked the sketches and shaped the group's visual language. Terry Gilliam, Wikipedia (read 2026-10-15)

He chose the theme, Sousa's Liberty Bell march, because it was in the public domain. The foot in the titles is cut from Cupid's foot in Bronzino's Venus, Cupid, Folly and Time. Monty Python's Flying Circus and List of Monty Python's Flying Circus episodes, Wikipedia (read 2026-10-15)

He appeared on screen too, usually in parts the others did not want, such as the knight who hits people with a chicken. Monty Python's Flying Circus and List of Monty Python's Flying Circus episodes, Wikipedia (read 2026-10-15)

His screen parts included Cardinal Fang of the Spanish Inquisition. Terry Gilliam, official Monty Python biography

He co-directed Monty Python and the Holy Grail (1975) with Terry Jones, was production designer on Life of Brian (1979), and made the short The Crimson Permanent Assurance for The Meaning of Life (1983). Terry Gilliam, official Monty Python biography

In 1978 he published Animations of Mortality, with Lucinda Cowell (Methuen), an illustrated, tongue-in-cheek, semi-autobiographical how-to book on his technique. Gilliam, Terry, Encyclopedia.com (Contemporary Authors)

A bookseller's description of Animations of Mortality says it starts from the walk cycle and gives ways to avoid moving legs at all. Animations of Mortality, bookseller listing (Stuart Ng Books)

An animation studies post describes him as an advocate of corner-cutting, recommending excuses such as long grass, darkness or roller skates instead of laborious animation. Animation Studies blog post citing Gilliam as an advocate of corner-cutting

Influences he names

He loved Disney's craft and the Grimm stories behind its films, and the European look of Disneyland. Alex Denney, The art that makes Terry Gilliam tick, Dazed, 10 October 2015

He loves the Surrealists because they make the mind work to make sense of them. He first heard of Max Ernst when a critic compared his Python animation to Ernst. Alex Denney, The art that makes Terry Gilliam tick, Dazed, 10 October 2015

He and Terry Jones call Bruegel and Bosch favourites. He finds the medieval view of the world more vivid than modern imagery. Terry Gilliam revela el mundo surrealista del arte Monty Python, Expansión / CNN México, 9 December 2010

He reportedly said the Holy Grail animations drew on a book about the illustrations in medieval manuscript margins. The title he gave has not been traced. Terry Gilliam animations that were left out of 'Monty Python and the Holy Grail', Dangerous Minds

In 2015 he also named Kubrick as a great hero, Dostoevsky, and Banksy. Alex Denney, The art that makes Terry Gilliam tick, Dazed, 10 October 2015

The feature films

After Holy Grail he directed Jabberwocky (1977), Time Bandits (1981), Brazil (1985), The Adventures of Baron Munchausen (1988), The Fisher King (1991), 12 Monkeys (1995), Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas (1998), The Brothers Grimm (2005), Tideland (2005), The Imaginarium of Doctor Parnassus (2009), The Zero Theorem (2013) and The Man Who Killed Don Quixote (2018). Terry Gilliam, Wikipedia (read 2026-10-15)

Brazil earned him a shared Oscar nomination for best original screenplay. Terry Gilliam, official Monty Python biography

His memoir, Gilliamesque: A Pre-Posthumous Memoir, written with Ben Thompson, came out from Canongate in 2015. We have not read it. Terry Gilliam with Ben Thompson, Gilliamesque: A Pre-Posthumous Memoir, Canongate, 2015 (ISBN 978-1-78211-106-1)

Limits of this biography

  • Most facts come from Wikipedia, the official Python biography and interviews retold by journalists. The three books that would settle the open points (canongate2015, sterritt2004, christie1999) have not been read.
  • Open points: whether We Have Ways of Making You Laugh held his first animation; why he left the US in his own words; the Black Spot's episode and the 'gangrene' redub; the Killer Cars episode and narrator; the title of the marginalia book.
  • His feature films are listed for context. This profile studies the cut-out animation, 1968-1983.
