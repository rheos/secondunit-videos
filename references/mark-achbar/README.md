# Mark Achbar reference profile

Built 2026-10-19 (issue #255). A study of how Mark Achbar's two landmark documentaries look and argue:
*Manufacturing Consent: Noam Chomsky and the Media* (1992, co-directed with Peter Wintonick) and *The
Corporation* (2003, co-directed with Jennifer Abbott, written by Joel Bakan). It exists so a video session
can ask "what would Achbar add to this shot?" and get a sourced answer.

Why now: Robin knows Mark and is pitching graphics and animation for his current film, *Romancing the
Atom* (Invisible Hand Productions, with Knowledge Network and TVO; director Mark Achbar, producer Betsy
Carson). The company site says the film is grounded in Robert Del Tredici's five decades of atomic
photography and draws a parallel between early nuclear weapons and the rise of AI [ihpi]. Achbar worked
with Del Tredici on *At Work in the Fields of the Bomb* in the 1980s, when he was "very concerned about
nuclear issues" [presskit, POV interview]. So the nuclear subject is not new to him.

## Rights: why there are no images here

Both films are copyrighted (Necessary Illusions and the NFB; Big Picture Media Corporation). This folder
holds no stills, frames or clips. It cites and links. Both films are streamable through the producers'
own links on [ihpi.ca](https://ihpi.ca), which is where to watch a device before building on it. The
library card in `references/index.html` uses our own drawing of the column-inch comparison, not a frame.

| | Manufacturing Consent (1992) | The Corporation (2003) |
|---|---|---|
| Directors | Mark Achbar, Peter Wintonick | Mark Achbar, Jennifer Abbott |
| Writing | Built from Chomsky's own talks and interviews | Joel Bakan; narration by Harold Crooks and Mark Achbar |
| Length | 167 min, two parts with an intermission [wiki-mc, hinson1993] | 145 min theatrical; 3 x 57:50 TV version and a 167 min festival cut [readme2006] |
| Editor | Peter Wintonick [nfb-mc] | Jennifer Abbott [presskit] |
| Graphics and animation | "Animation": Joan Churchill, Mark Achbar, Brian Duchscherer, Katharine Assals, Wendy Tilby [nfb-mc, tiff-cfe] | "Our brilliant graphic team at Bento Box" designed the psychopath checklist [presskit] |
| Music | Carl Schultz [nfb-mc] | Original music Leonard J. Paul; sound designer and music supervisor Velcrow Ripper; tracks by Yo La Tengo, Loscil, Do Make Say Think and others [presskit] |
| Archive | About 185 archival sources [npr1993]; 120 hours of Chomsky footage over 25 years [hinson1993] | Archival researcher Paula Sawadsky; "footage from pop culture, advertising, TV news, and corporate propaganda" [readme2006] |
| Distributor | Zeitgeist Films | Zeitgeist Films |

Who did which graphic in *Manufacturing Consent* is not documented in anything we read. The NFB credit
list is the only crew source. Wendy Tilby is the NFB animator of *Strings* and *When the Day Breaks*;
that she animated a given sequence here is not established. "Bento Box" in the *Corporation* press kit is
not identified further. Do not assume it is the Los Angeles studio Bento Box Entertainment (INFERENCE: the
names match but nothing links them).

## What the filmmakers say they were doing

Two statements carry most of the weight, and both are documented:

- Achbar on *Manufacturing Consent* (NPR, 1993): the screens, surgeons and staging remind viewers they
  are watching a media product, so they think about the medium as much as the message [npr1993].
- Achbar's statement for *The Corporation*: his aim was "to make the commonplace seem strange" and give
  viewers critical distance from corporate culture [presskit]. Abbott says her own work tries to make
  accepted social conventions "appear strange" [presskit, POV interview].

Everything in `AGENT-DIRECTION.txt` builds on those two sentences.

## Fact and inference

Every device in `devices.md` cites what a source says happens on screen. The "Reading" line under each is
ours and says INFERENCE. We did not watch the films for this build, so:

- No timestamps are given for *Manufacturing Consent*. None of our sources gave any.
- For *The Corporation* we have the official 23-chapter DVD list [dvd-chapters] and tape timecodes from
  Zeitgeist's study guide [zeitgeist-sg]. Those timecodes (for example 11:21:42:12 for "Triumph of the
  Shill") are master-tape numbers with an unknown offset, not running times. Treat them as order, not
  position.
- Where two sources describe what may be the same scene (the column-inch rolls in a stadium, MC02, and a
  whole *Times* laid out on a gymnasium floor, MC11), they are kept as separate entries.
- Several details often repeated about these films did not check out or could not be confirmed. They are
  listed under "Not verified" at the end of `devices.md`.

Watch the scene before using a device as a visual anchor. Quotes from sources are kept under 15 words.
