# Study: an Achbar pass on the Romancing the Atom test reel

The reel is `mv-projects/achbar-atom-reel` (issue #252): a 60 s spec piece of seven plates in a
blueprint-schematic look, cut to the glassy-art-pop score. Plate list and sources are in that folder's
`BUILD-STATE.md` (read for this study, not edited). This file asks of each plate: what would the devices in
`devices.md` add? Every suggestion is our proposal (INFERENCE), not Achbar's. Device IDs point to the
catalogue.

## What the film is, from its producer

"Romancing the Atom examines the ethical, environmental, and political consequences of nuclear weapons"
and is grounded in Robert Del Tredici's photographs; it draws a parallel between early nuclear weapons and
the rise of AI [ihpi.ca]. Two things follow for a graphics pitch (INFERENCE):

- Del Tredici's photographs will be the film's spine, the way Chomsky's broadcasts were for Manufacturing
  Consent (MC12). Graphics should frame and measure his photographs, not compete with them. Our reel's
  rule that archival photos are never recoloured (paper mount, push-in, iris only) already fits. Leave a
  slot that shows we know his work is the centre. Rights to his photos go through the production; we do
  not use them in a spec piece.
- The atom/AI parallel is an argument with two columns. Achbar's strongest devices (MC02, TC01) are
  two-column devices. The reel already leans that way, which is the best thing about it.

## Plate by plate

### Plate 0, title (0-9.1 s): atom, heart built round the nucleus, ROMANCING / the atom
- **Claim:** we have been in love with this technology.
- **Achbar pass:** TC02, the repeated phrase. The title says "romancing"; the archive is full of the
  language of romance about the atom (and now AI). A 3-4 s montage of the phrase, in type, harvested from
  sourced quotes, before the heart forms. Option B, TC03: two or three short metaphors for the atom set
  against the same number for AI, sourced, in the type style.
- **Recommend:** keep the title as built. If anything, add TC02 as a type-only pre-title only once we have
  sourced lines. Do not invent quotes to fill it.

### Plate 1, the promise (9.1-20.1 s): Atoms for Peace stamp, Strauss 1954, network, Altman 2025
- **Claim:** the same promise, "too cheap to meter", made twice, seventy years apart.
- **Achbar pass:** this is already MC05 and TC06 in spirit (the institution's own confident words). Two
  additions: (a) set the two quotes as identical type on two strips of equal width, then reveal the only
  words that differ ("electrical energy" vs "intelligence"), so the rhyme is measured, not asserted (MC02
  logic applied to text). (b) MC05: show each quote in its medium, a 1954 newspaper column and a 2025 blog
  page, rather than on clean blueprint, so the viewer sees the channel.
- **Recommend:** (a). It is cheap, it stays in the look, and the payoff is a visible match.

### Plate 2, the same lab (20.1-28.3 s): Trinity, then ENIAC and "a secret problem from Los Alamos"
- **Claim:** the computer and the bomb grew up in the same building.
- **Achbar pass:** TC01's "case history" frame. Present Trinity and ENIAC as two matched case files
  (date, place, sponsor, what it was for), filled in field by field, same form for both. The match of the
  fields is the argument. Keep the BUILD-STATE caution: the film may say only "a secret problem from Los
  Alamos", so the "what it was for" field on ENIAC stays redacted, which is itself a good image (MC01 has
  the same instinct: show the editing).
- **Recommend:** the redacted field. One black bar that never lifts says "classified" better than a line
  of type.

### Plate 3, the scope (28.3-39.2 s): the real warhead curve, peak 70,374 in 1986, 12,187 in 2026
- **Claim:** the stockpile rose to 70,000 and is still 12,000.
- **Achbar pass:** MC02, the measure made physical. Instead of only a trace, let the curve unspool as a
  paper strip off a roll, with a scale tick for every thousand warheads, the strip running out of frame
  at the 1986 peak while lights come on along it; then the number arrives last. The 2026 figure is the
  strip that is still on the floor. Option B: MC03, put the curve on a public screen (a stock ticker or
  scoreboard) among ordinary numbers.
- **Recommend:** MC02. It is the device Achbar's films are best remembered for, and the warhead series
  is exactly the kind of sourced ratio it needs. Keep the oscilloscope look; the strip can be the scope's
  paper chart recorder.

### Plate 4, the clock (39.2-47.4 s): 1947 seven minutes, sweep, 85 seconds
- **Claim:** we are closer to midnight than ever, and the Bulletin now names AI.
- **Achbar pass:** MC03 and MC04. Put the clock on a public screen (a Times Square-style billboard or a
  stadium scoreboard) between an ad and a sports score, with the crowd not looking up. Wintonick said they
  put Chomsky where people expect commercials and scores; the shoppers mostly ignored him (npr1993). That
  indifference is the point of this plate too. Keep the reel's honesty note: our clock is our own drawing,
  and the sweep does not claim the clock moved only one way.
- **Recommend:** a single cut-away to the scoreboard version for two bars, then back to the blueprint.
  An accent, not a rebuild.

### Plate 5, red flags (47.4-55.6 s): FORM RF-1, two columns (THE ATOM, 1945 / AI, 2025), SOUND FAMILIAR?
- **Claim:** the AI story repeats the atom's warning signs.
- **Achbar pass:** this plate already is TC01, a diagnostic form with check marks landing one by one.
  What Achbar's version had that ours lacks: real criteria and an outside authority. The Corporation used
  actual WHO and DSM-IV criteria and had Robert Hare sign off (presskit). Our rows ("PROMISED THE WORLD",
  "TOO CHEAP TO METER", "AN ARMS RACE", "CAN'T BE UN-INVENTED") are editorial, and BUILD-STATE says so.
  Two fixes: (a) give each row a tiny source line in each column (a dated quote or record for the atom and
  for AI), so every tick is earned; or (b) take the rows from a published framework (for example the
  Bulletin's own statement) and cite it on the form header. Keep the first tick on a beat: in Achbar's
  account that is where the audience laughed.
- **Recommend:** (a) for the pitch. "TOO CHEAP TO METER" is already sourced from plate 1. The other three
  need sources before this goes to Mark; he is the director whose film got its laugh from real criteria.

### Plate 6, title block (55.6-60 s): colophon
- **Claim:** this is a test, made by us, for him.
- **Achbar pass:** MC06, show the strings. The colophon can carry one honest piece of the making (the
  sources list scrolling in the title block, or the scene's own frame number), which reads as "we know how
  your films work". TC12 and MC14: both films end on what a viewer can do; Invisible Hand's site carries a
  global appeal to the nine nuclear-armed governments. A final line pointing there would echo that, but
  only if Mark wants it in a pitch piece. Ask before adding.
- **Recommend:** sources in the title block. No call to action without his say.

## If we do only three things

1. Plate 5: source every row (TC01's real criteria). This is the plate Mark will judge hardest.
2. Plate 3: unroll the warhead curve as a strip (MC02). The most Achbar move available to us.
3. Plate 4: one cut-away of the clock on a public screen nobody watches (MC03).

These keep the reel's look, add no paid generation, and each one ties to a documented device he made.
